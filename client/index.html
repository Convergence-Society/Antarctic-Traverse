The Operational Challenge

Research vessels operating near Antarctica navigate against sea ice that shifts by the hour and icebergs that drift for weeks. Current methodologies rely on fragmented telemetry and manual analysis.


Antarctic Traverse integrates satellite, sea-ice, ocean, and weather data to forecast ice conditions, detect icebergs, and predict their movement. These forecasts are mathematically transformed into dynamic hazard maps, delivering one unified view of hazards, route risks, and safer alternatives.

Algorithmic Methodology

A six-stage pipeline translating raw Copernicus and NSIDC datasets into Pareto-optimal navigational vectors.

Phase I

Spatial-Temporal Forecasting

The system employs U-Net for precise iceberg segmentation and ConvLSTM for short-term spatial and temporal sea-ice dynamics, trained against historical radar backscatter and climate variables.

Phase II

Data Assimilation

Continuous ingestion and registration of Sentinel-1 SAR, AMSR2, ERA5, and GLORYS12V1 ocean data onto a common spatial grid.

Phase III

Risk Quantification

A POLARIS-style Risk Index Outcome (RIO) evaluates hazards, dynamically mapping AI predictive confidence to actionable maritime constraints.

Phase IV

Multi-Objective Optimization

A* and Dijkstra algorithms conduct primary route searches over the time-varying graph. Subsequently, NSGA-II frameworks process the Pareto front to balance fuel efficiency, transit time, and operational risk.

System Architecture

Engineered for high availability and computational scale. The infrastructure separates heavy tensor operations from rapid client delivery, ensuring research vessels receive deterministic routing data in real-time.
